Spending time with a character

Read a character story with an ear for how the people in it talk to each other.

오늘 어때 includes stories centered on individual characters. Inside a character story, a conversation is a chance to spend time with the person across from you. What they notice, what they bring up, and how they answer can give you something to carry into the next scene.

Listen for what they notice

Picture two people stopping on the same street. One brings up a shop they remember. The other waits until the street gets quiet to finish what they were saying. Both details are easy to picture. Each makes you curious about the person who chose to pay attention to it. This is an example of what to listen for in a character story.

When a character mentions something small, give the detail a little room. It can tell you what their attention returns to when they have time to talk. A line may sound casual while you are reading it and stay in your head long after the scene has moved on.

Make space for a first impression to change

You may meet a character whose first line strikes you as formal. As you read on, a warm response might make that formality seem like care. A person who gets a laugh out of you early on might also have moments when you want to take them seriously. How you read the early lines can change as you spend more time with the character.

If you find yourself changing your mind, see if you can place the moment that did it. Sometimes the shift starts with a single response. Sometimes you simply realize that you can hear the person's way of speaking more clearly than you did at the beginning. Your first impression and your later one can both help you remember the story.

Think about the conversation afterward

Once you have finished reading, try putting into words what you remember about the character. Start with a detail that came from the story, such as what you heard in one of their replies. A description like “I liked the way they took that question seriously” gives you a specific moment to think about. It also gives someone else an easy way into a conversation about the story.

If you read another character's story later, try the same thing. The detail may come to you in a different place. One person's style may be clear from the way they start talking. Another person's way of answering may only stand out when you think back. How each person comes across can give two dates quite different moods.

There is room to enjoy a character story for a single exchange, or to think back on the way the whole conversation felt. When a line stays with you, consider what you learned about the person who said it. That is often the part you will want to talk about later.
